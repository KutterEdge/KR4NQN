Solar & Geomagnetic Data — Simple Rules of Thumb

For HF propagation, these are simple rules of thumb:

SFI — Solar Flux Index

  • SFI 70–90: Low — higher HF bands such as 15, 12, and 10 meters may be limited.
  • SFI 90–120: Fair to good.
  • SFI 120–150: Good — higher bands become much more useful.
  • SFI 150–200: Very good — often excellent 10–20 meter propagation.
  • SFI 200+: Excellent potential, assuming geomagnetic conditions are quiet.

304A — EUV Flux (He II line)

  • 304A below 85: Weak — EUV ionization running light; higher HF bands (17–10m) may lag what SFI alone suggests.
  • 304A 85–110: Fair — typical mid-cycle EUV output.
  • 304A 110–130: Good — solid ionizing EUV output, supporting higher-band openings.
  • 304A 130+: Excellent — strong EUV output, often near solar-cycle maximum conditions.

304A is the Sun's actual ionizing extreme-ultraviolet output (the He II emission line, measured by the GOES SEM instrument) — the radiation that directly creates and sustains the ionosphere's F2 layer. SFI (10.7 cm radio flux) is a well-correlated proxy for the same solar activity, not this same radiation; the two normally track together, but 304A is the more direct read on ionization. Our HF band-condition grid uses it as a small confirming nudge alongside SFI, mainly on the 17–10 meter bands.

A-index — Lower Is Better

  • A 0–7: Quiet — generally very good HF conditions.
  • A 8–15: Unsettled — usually still workable, with some fading or weaker paths.
  • A 16–29: Active — HF propagation may be noticeably degraded.
  • A 30–49: Stormy — poor HF conditions are likely, especially on higher-latitude paths.
  • A 50+: Major geomagnetic disturbance — HF can be severely degraded.

K-index — Lower Is Better

  • K 0–1: Quiet — excellent for HF propagation.
  • K 2: Good — generally favorable HF conditions.
  • K 3: Unsettled — some paths may become weaker or less stable.
  • K 4: Active — HF propagation may be noticeably disturbed.
  • K 5+: Geomagnetic storm — HF can be poor, especially on polar and higher-latitude paths.

Remember: For SFI and 304A, higher is generally better. For the A-index and K-index, lower is generally better. These are quick operating guides, not guarantees; actual propagation also depends on time of day, frequency, location, path, season, and current solar activity.
